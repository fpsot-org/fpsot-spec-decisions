= Reasons and explanations
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

Every regime that the research examined gives the person some right to know why.
They differ in depth.
Québec guidance speaks of the reasons and the main factors and parameters, with their relative weight.
French law speaks of the parameters as they applied to the case of the person.
The Court of Justice of the European Union asks for the procedure and the principles that the controller actually applied.

The most tested model of reasons is not governmental.
link:https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/9/[Regulation B] of the United States asks lenders for specific principal reasons that describe the factors actually scored.
It rejects vague reasons, such as a failure to meet internal standards.
Its official commentary says that more than four reasons is not likely to help the applicant.

This page turns those duties into codes and values, which a service renders into a notice in each language.

== Reason codes

[[DEC-050]]
*DEC-050.* Each reason code MUST be a concept of a concept scheme that meets the vocabularies specification.
The scheme gives each code its labels and definitions in English and in French, under VOC-010 and VOC-015.
A code never changes and never moves to another concept, under VOC-007.
Each record MUST name the release of the scheme that its codes come from, under VOC-036.
A code MUST name a factor of the decision, such as `household-income-above-limit`.

The file `examples/reason-codes.jsonld` is a small reason scheme.
Its codes describe the facts that drove the result, not the result itself.

[[DEC-051]]
*DEC-051.* A reason code MUST NOT state only a verdict or a general failure.
Codes such as `not-eligible`, `does-not-meet-criteria`, `internal-policy` or `score-too-low` do not conform.

A person cannot contest a reason that names no fact.

[[DEC-052]]
*DEC-052.* A record MUST give its reasons in order of importance, with a rank from 1.
Each reason MUST say whether it is principal, and MUST cite the provision that its rule element applies.
Each reason SHOULD give the value of the factor and the threshold that the rule compared it with.
These values come from the inputs and from the dated parameters of DEC-037.

A notice in any language and at any depth can then come from the same record.

[[DEC-053]]
*DEC-053.* A record SHOULD mark at most four reasons as principal.
A notice SHOULD give the principal reasons first.
When a profile sets `allReasons`, the explanation MUST give every reason of the record.

The `fr` profile sets `allReasons`, since French law speaks of the parameters as they applied to the case.
A long list of reasons hides the ones that matter.

[[DEC-054]]
*DEC-054.* A reason scheme MAY hold the code `other`.
Only a person in a review step MAY give that code, and a rule provider MUST NOT.
A reason with the code `other` MUST carry text in English and in French in `otherText`.

A rule provider knows every reason that its rules can produce, so it never needs `other`.
A reviewer may meet a case that no code covers.
The text in both languages lets the body render the notice in the language of the person.

== Counterfactuals

[[DEC-055]]
*DEC-055.* For each principal reason, a rule provider SHOULD give the exact value at which the reason stops applying.
It assumes that the other facts stay the same.
The value MUST be exact, such as a threshold from the parameters, and never an estimate.
The reason then carries `counterfactualRelation`, such as `at-most`, and `counterfactualValue`.

Rules can compute such a value exactly, which learning models cannot.
The Court of Justice of the European Union points to explanations of this kind in case C-203/22.
So does the principle of explainability in the Québec statement of principles on artificial intelligence.
This rule is a SHOULD in version 0.1.
A later release of a profile may make it a MUST.

== Notices

[[DEC-056]]
*DEC-056.* A decision record MUST NOT hold rendered prose.
A service MUST render each notice when it issues it, from the record and a versioned template.
It MUST use the official language that the person chose.
It MUST keep a notice record of the class `Notice`.
That record names the template, its release, the language, the channel and the time.
The body MUST seal the rendered notice under the signed records specification, and name its seal manifest under LOG-019.

The texts of DEC-054 and DEC-062 are the only text that a person writes in these records.
One record then serves a notice in English, a notice in French and a deeper explanation on request.
The sealed notice keeps the exact text that the person received.

[[DEC-057]]
*DEC-057.* Each notice MUST meet the notice controls of the profiles of its decision type.

* `automationNotice`: the notice states that automation took part in the decision, and how much.
* `recourseNotice`: the notice gives each recourse or observations channel, with its contact and its deadline.

The notice record MUST state whether the notice said that automation took part.
It MUST list each channel that the notice offered.

Québec guidance asks the body to inform the person at the latest when it gives the decision.
It also asks for the means to send observations to a staff member who can review the decision.

== General explanation

[[DEC-058]]
*DEC-058.* A deploying body SHOULD explain each decision type in a published text, in plain language and in both languages.
It SHOULD give the main factors and their relative weight, the degree of automation and the recourse.
It SHOULD also link to the rule source of DEC-038.

A person can then understand the rules before applying, and an outside reviewer can check them.

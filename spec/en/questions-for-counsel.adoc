= Questions for counsel
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

This page is informative.
It sets no rule, and it is not legal advice.
It lists the legal questions that this specification raises but cannot answer.
A lawyer must answer each one before {abbr-fpsot-first} or a deploying body relies on it.
Some sources refuse automated readers, so the research read several statutes through guidance or secondary sources.
A text or an article number marked _to be verified_ comes from such a source, not from the law itself.

== Scope of the laws

1. *Rules engines under the federal directive.* Is a deterministic rules engine that does not learn an automated decision system?
The question concerns the definition in the 2025 text of the Directive on Automated Decision-Making.
What changed in the text of 24 June 2025, _to be verified_?
The points of interest are the levels of Appendix C and the content of explanations.
Another is the proposed duty to document human overrides.

2. *Rules engines as artificial intelligence.* Is a deterministic rules engine an AI system under the 2025 Québec statement of principles?
That statement follows the definition of the OECD.
Is it one under the European regulation on artificial intelligence?
If not, which Québec instruments govern it, beyond article 65.2 of the Access Act and the Act respecting administrative justice?

3. *Administrative justice.* Do articles 2, 4, 5 and 8 of the Act respecting administrative justice apply here, _to be verified_?
Do they apply to every automated decision on a benefit or on eligibility?
Does the duty of prior notice in article 5 reach beyond permits and other authorizations?

4. *Express authority.* After Robodebt, some reviews called for express statutory authority before a body automates a decision.
Does a federal or Québec body that uses the {abbr-fpsot} reference rules, or an override, need such authority?

5. *International remedies.* Has Canada ratified the Council of Europe Framework Convention on artificial intelligence, CETS 225?
Would its duty to provide remedies bind federal or provincial bodies that use {abbr-fpsot} software?

== Article 65.2 of the Québec Access Act

1. *Wording.* What is the exact text of article 65.2, _to be verified_?
The article asks for notice at the latest when the body informs the person of the decision.
What does that timing ask of software?
Who counts as a staff member who can review the decision?

2. *Real human effect.* Which human actions mean that a Québec decision no longer rests only on automated processing?
Is a recorded confirmation with a justification enough, as DEC-062 records it?
Or must the reviewer show that they examined the inputs?

3. *Personal information used.* A body must give the personal information that it used, on request.
Must it then keep the input values for the life of the decision?
Or can it rely on references to source systems, which may themselves change or destroy the values?
DEC-042 assumes the first answer.

4. *Withheld factors.* May a body withhold some main factors as protected technical information when the rules are open source?
Does publication under DEC-038 waive that exception?

== Records and retention

1. *Federal retention floor.* The Privacy Act and its regulations set a retention floor, _to be verified_.
The sections at stake are section 6 of the act and section 4 of the regulations.
That floor would be two years after the last administrative use.
Are the rule version, the parameters and the trace personal information used for an administrative purpose?
Does that floor then apply to them as well?

2. *Language of the notice.* Consider a Québec body under the Charter of the French language.
Must it give the notice and the explanation in French first?
Does that hold even when the person chose English?
Does rendering from codes at notice time, under DEC-056, meet any duty to keep the version that the body sent?

== Reviewers and employees

1. *Engagement evidence.* Can a body capture the time that a reviewer spends and whether they opened the explanation, under DEC-065?
Can it do so under the Access Act and labor law without a separate privacy impact assessment?
Can it do so without a consultation with the unions?

== Liability of the steward

1. *Overrides that fail.* An adopter may override an {abbr-fpsot} rule, and the override may produce unlawful decisions.
What liability or harm to its reputation does {abbr-fpsot} then face as steward of the default rule and suite?
Should the results of the suite carry a disclaimer that they are not a certification, as TST-047 already states?

== Case law

1. *Algorithm-assisted triage.* Sources disagree on the outcome of Haghshenas v. Canada, 2023 FC 464, on the use of the Chinook tool, _to be verified_.
Does any case set a standard for the reasons of a decision that a rules engine helped make?

= Jurisdiction profiles
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

The laws on automated decisions differ in depth, not in kind.
Each asks for a notice, an explanation, a human channel, an impact assessment and a record.
They differ on when a person must take part and on how deep the explanation goes.
They also differ on which assessment comes first.
This page keeps those differences in profiles, apart from the core rules.
A change in a law then becomes a new release of a profile, not a change to the contract.

The research read several of these laws through government guidance or secondary sources.
Each profile file names the texts that it follows and marks the ones that are _to be verified_.
A profile states the practice of {abbr-fpsot-first}, and it is not legal advice.

== Decision types

[[DEC-010]]
*DEC-010.* A deploying body MUST describe each decision type in a file of the class `DecisionType` of `model/decisions.yaml`.
The description MUST give the profiles that apply with their releases, the impact level and the legal authority.
It MUST also give the reason scheme, the rule set and the review settings.
The body MUST publish a new release of the description for each change, under Semantic Versioning 2.0.0.
Each decision record names the release that applied, under DEC-040.

The file `examples/decision-type.yaml` describes a fictional housing benefit.

== The profiles

[[DEC-011]]
*DEC-011.* A decision type MUST declare the `core` profile, and each profile of this table whose law applies.
The decision service MUST apply every control of each declared profile at the impact level of the decision type.
Each profile is a file of the class `Profile` in the folder `profiles/`.
When two profiles set the same control, the stricter setting applies:

* `humanReview`, `dualReview` and `allReasons`: `true` is stricter than `false`.
* `automationNotice` and `recourseNotice`: `required`, then `required-when-fully-automated`, then `recommended`, from the strictest.
* Impact assessments add up: the decision type needs each one that a declared profile names, under DEC-014.

[cols="2,3,3,4",options="header"]
|===
|Profile |Applies to |Impact assessment |Main controls
|`core` |Every decision type |None named |The core rules only. A notice of automation and a recourse channel are recommended.
|`ca-federal` |Federal institutions of Canada under the Directive on Automated Decision-Making |Algorithmic Impact Assessment |A notice of automation and recourse at every level. A review step before a final result at levels 3 and 4.
|`ca-qc` |Québec public bodies |Privacy impact assessment, under article 63.5 of the Access Act, _to be verified_ |A notice when the decision is fully automated. A recourse and observations channel on every notice.
|`fr` |French administrations |None named in this release |An explicit mention of algorithmic processing on every decision. Every reason in the explanation.
|`eu-ai-high-risk` |High-risk systems of the European regulation on artificial intelligence |Fundamental rights impact assessment |A notice of automation. The override and stop functions of DEC-063, which apply under every profile.
|===

The `ca-federal` profile follows the proposed text of the federal directive, which a 2024 document of the {abbr-tbs-first} sets out.
At levels 3 and 4, that text keeps the final decision for a person, at defined points of human intervention.
Secondary sources date a later amendment to 24 June 2025, and its content is _to be verified_.

The `ca-qc` profile follows link:https://www.quebec.ca/gouvernement/travailler-gouvernement/normes-gouvernance-pratiques-internes/protection-des-renseignements-personnels/technologie-et-droit-a-la-protection-des-renseignements-personnels/decision-traitement-automatise[government guidance on article 65.2] of the Québec Access Act.
That article covers a decision based only on the automated processing of personal information.
The guidance says that a minor human step without real effect leaves a decision fully automated.
The wording of the article and article 8 of the Act respecting administrative justice are _to be verified_.

The `fr` profile follows link:https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000033205535[article L311-3-1] of the French code on relations between the public and the administration.
That article asks for an explicit mention on each individual decision that rests on algorithmic processing.
Article R311-3-1-2 lists what the person can obtain on request, and its items 2 to 4 are _to be verified_.

The `eu-ai-high-risk` profile follows the link:https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj[European regulation on artificial intelligence].
Its Annex III counts a system that evaluates eligibility for public assistance benefits as high-risk, _to be verified_.
Whether a deterministic rules engine is an artificial intelligence system under that regulation is a question for counsel.
A body adopts this profile when its counsel says that the regulation applies, or by choice.

== Releases of a profile

[[DEC-012]]
*DEC-012.* Each profile MUST have its own version number under Semantic Versioning 2.0.0, apart from the version of this specification.
A profile MUST NOT remove a result kind of DEC-024, or relax a MUST of the core rules.
A release that removes or relaxes a control of the same profile MUST take a new major version.
A decision record MUST name the release of each profile that applied, under DEC-040.

Laws on automated decisions are still moving, as the 2025 federal text and the European revisions show.
A profile release can follow such a change without breaking the contract that every rule provider implements.

== Impact levels

[[DEC-013]]
*DEC-013.* A decision type MUST state one impact level, from `level-1` to `level-4`.
Under the `ca-federal` profile, the level MUST be the level of its published Algorithmic Impact Assessment.
Under the other profiles, the deploying body MUST set the level from its own impact assessment.
The level, and never the rule provider, decides which controls apply.

The link:https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/responsible-use-ai/algorithmic-impact-assessment.html[federal assessment tool] scores the risk and grades it in four levels.
Mitigation does not lower the level, and only a new assessment can change it.
Québec and France set no levels, so their profiles apply the same controls at each level.

== Impact assessments

[[DEC-014]]
*DEC-014.* A decision type MUST name each impact assessment that its profiles ask for, with its reference and its date.
A decision service MUST NOT run a decision type in production while such an assessment is missing.
The deploying body MUST review the assessment when the scope or the rules of the decision type change.

The service can check this rule from the description alone, before the first decision.
The content of each assessment stays the duty of the deploying body.

= Human review
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

Several regimes ask for a person in the loop, and they agree that a token step does not count.
Québec guidance says that a minor human step without real effect leaves a decision fully automated.
Under the European regulation on artificial intelligence, a person must be able to override the output of a high-risk system.
This reading of article 14 is _to be verified_.
At its two highest levels, the proposed text of the federal directive keeps the final decision for a person.

People also tend to trust a machine too much.
A link:https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3240751[review of 74 studies] on automation bias found errors both of omission and of commission.
A record can only show real human effect if each human step is its own fact, with its own evidence.

== Degree of automation

[[DEC-060]]
*DEC-060.* A decision service MUST derive the degree of automation for each record from the review steps behind it.
It MUST NOT take the degree from a setting of the decision type.

* `fully-automated`: no review step led to the record.
* `human-confirmed`: each review step kept the result that the service showed, with a justification.
* `human-decided`: at least one review step changed the result or set it.

A step that only refers the case or asks for a missing fact neither keeps nor changes the result.
It does not count toward the degree.

A label chosen in advance cannot prove what happened to one case.
The check `examples/check-examples.py` recomputes the degree from the review steps.

== Gating

[[DEC-061]]
*DEC-061.* A profile level or a decision type can ask for human review.
Then the service MUST NOT return a final `decided` result without a review step that kept or changed the result.
Until a person acts, the result MUST be `referred`, with the reason `human-review-required`.
The record then keeps the outcome that the rules proposed in `recommendedOutcome`.
The final record MUST name the review steps that led to it, and revise the referred record under DEC-043.
Each review step MUST be about a record that the final record revises.

The service applies this rule, so no rule provider can skip it.
The files `examples/decision-record-1.json`, `examples/review-step.json` and `examples/decision-record-2.json` show the three records of one gated decision.

== Review steps

[[DEC-062]]
*DEC-062.* Each review step MUST be a record of the class `ReviewStep` of `model/decisions.yaml`, apart from the decision record.
It MUST hold the reviewer as the actor of IAM-091, and the party that the reviewer acts for, under IAM-064.
It MUST hold the authority of the reviewer, what the service showed, the action and whether the reviewer agreed.
It MUST hold a justification in the words of the reviewer, with its language, and the times of the step.
The service MUST NOT pre-fill or offer a template for the justification.
A change or a disagreement MUST carry an override reason from the reason scheme.

The identity specification owns the actor and the represented party, and this rule takes them as they are.
A justification and the list of what the service showed tell a real review from a click.
A step that lacks them does not conform, and the service cannot count it under DEC-060.

[[DEC-063]]
*DEC-063.* A reviewer with the authority MUST be able to set aside, change or reverse the result shown.
An authorized role MUST be able to stop automated decisions of a decision type, while cases still reach people.
Each such stop and restart MUST be an audit event under the observability specification.

Observations, requests for reconsideration and appeals MUST each lead to a review step.
A changed or a confirmed result then becomes a new record under DEC-043.

== Dual review

[[DEC-064]]
*DEC-064.* A decision type or a profile level MAY ask for dual review.
Under dual review, a final `decided` result MUST follow two review steps by two different actors.
The service MUST check that the actors differ, and MUST NOT accept a second step from the same person.

The European regulation asks two people to confirm the results of some biometric systems.
A body can use the same control for decisions with a high impact.

== Engagement evidence

[[DEC-065]]
*DEC-065.* A review step SHOULD record whether the reviewer opened the inputs and the reasons.
It SHOULD also record how long the record stayed in view.
A deploying body MUST cover this evidence in its privacy impact assessment before it collects it.
It MUST NOT use this evidence to rate one employee, unless its assessment and its labor agreements allow it.

This evidence helps a body show that its human step had real effect.
It is also a form of monitoring of employees.
In Québec, it may call for an assessment under the Access Act and a talk with the unions.
That is a question for counsel.

== Override statistics

[[DEC-066]]
*DEC-066.* A deploying body MUST be able to count how often reviewers agree, change or reverse results.
It MUST count them for each decision type and rule version.
It MUST also count observations, reconsiderations and appeals, and how often they change the result.
It MUST compute these counts from the attributes of the business events of DEC-071.
The counts MUST NOT hold an identifier of a person, under DEC-044.
A reviewer appears as a role, under OBS-075, and never by name.

The proposed text of the federal directive asks institutions to document and analyze human overrides.
That point is _to be verified_ against the 2025 text.
High override rates show a weak rule, and rates near zero can show automation bias.
